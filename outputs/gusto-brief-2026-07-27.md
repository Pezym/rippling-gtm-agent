## 1. TL;DR

- **Gusto is executing a fortress-SMB strategy — deliberately narrowing while Rippling broadens.** The "People Platform" category they invented in 2015 appears quietly dropped for plain "all-in-one payroll and HR platform" — an inference from the current homepage plus third-party listings, since archives were unreachable [C002][C019] — first-party international EOR was exited on May 1, 2026 with new global customers handed to partner Remote [C041], the segment architecture tops out at "Growing businesses" with no mid-market or enterprise page [C017], and every featured customer story runs 7 to 61 employees [C016]. They are conceding the ground Rippling grows into.
- **They are winning where they play, and the momentum is real.** They announced $1B in trailing-12-month revenue on May 7, 2026 [C065], claim 500,000+ customers [C006] adding roughly 50,000 per quarter by their own count [C064], report that more than half of ARR now comes from products beyond payroll [C066], and hold roughly 12,848 G2 reviews at 4.6 stars [C058]. Do not attack the product love or the micro-SMB price position head-on.
- **Their attack on Rippling is pricing transparency plus simplicity — and it has three refutable seams.** The vs-Rippling page promises a sub-7-day switch [C009] and paints the alternative as "clunky, outdated systems full of add-ons and hidden fees" [C010]; but the comparison data is self-disclosed as "collected as of July 2025" [C011], the claim that "Rippling doesn't currently offer next day or 2nd day payroll" is refutable against Rippling's own published same-day payroll capability [C012], and "no surprise fees" collides with Gusto's own add-on sheet and tier-gate math [C045][C042].

- **The window to move is now.** Their channel mix is brand-heavy — roughly 76% of TV airings in the last 30 days are Oakland Roots community-sponsorship spots [C021] — performance creative is minimal-copy UI demos [C028], and the job postings sampled show no enterprise sales cluster (an absence read, not a re-fetched census [C076]). But an April 2025 GTM leadership build-out [C057] and an IPO-adjacent posture [C067] both say they will spend louder soon.

## 2. Messaging angles & themes they're running

The master message is simplification with warmth. The live homepage hero reads "Payroll, HR, Benefits. Simplified." over a membership-framed subhead — "Join 500,000+ small and medium-sized businesses that take care of their people with Gusto" — and a free-account CTA [C001]. The verbs do identity work: you *join* Gusto, and you do it to *take care of your people*. The LinkedIn self-description makes the identity explicit: "On a mission to grow the small business economy with technology and heart. Trusted by 500K+ businesses." [C050]. The 500K figure is their own, uncorroborated, and repeated across every surface [C006].

Five recurring angles carry the program:

**Effortlessness and relief.** The payroll page H1 is "Fast, accurate, and effortless payroll." with Instant Pay framed as funds landing "within minutes," and included tax filing framed as a jab — "Other providers charge for this — with us, it's included." [C015]. TV pairs customer-testimonial spots ("Kirsten: Payroll," "Heritage Bikes and Coffee") with pain-relief humor ("Too Much on Your Plate?", "Root Canal," "Time Is Money") [C023]. The documented Meta creative style is the product UI itself — a payroll dashboard with a Run Payroll button — under four words of copy: "Payroll just got easier" [C028].

**Speed as a customer-voiced stat.** Their recurring social proof point is "Average time to run payroll with Gusto? Our customers say 11 minutes" [C029].

**Proof by badge and self-survey.** Page titles lead with awards — "Payroll Software & Services for Small Businesses | Gusto Ranked #1 on G2 Fall 2025" [C033], and the construction segment page title carries "#1 in Satisfaction Score" [C032]. On-page proof is own-survey stat bands: "9 out of 10 customers would recommend Gusto," "152 hrs/year average time saved," "4.5/5 stars," footnoted to a survey of 538 Gusto customers from September 2024 — internal research presented as benchmark [C005].

**Transparent pricing as the attack line.** Against Rippling specifically: "As opposed to Rippling, we offer simple, transparent plans with no surprise fees" [C011], "Don't get caught using clunky, outdated systems full of add-ons and hidden fees" [C010], "Thinking of switching from Rippling? Make the move to Gusto in less than 7 days" [C009], and a flat factual assertion — "Rippling doesn't currently offer next day or 2nd day payroll" [C012].

**Segment and identity keywords.** A documented Facebook ad runs the headline "HR for Remote Teams" [C027], and the segment landing-page architecture enumerates the ICPs they buy: construction, franchise, financial services, wholesale distribution, restaurants, dental, healthcare, startups [C031].

The newest theme is AI: CEO Josh Reeves has made it the official growth narrative — "AI is enabling us to dramatically accelerate our roadmap and expand what we can do for small businesses" [C073] — expressed through the "Gusto Cofounder" launch push [C053] and an "AI teammate" YouTube demo [C054].

One tonal fact matters for how Rippling should respond: across the entire sampled social output there are zero competitor call-outs — the voice is warm, SMB-celebratory, and self-referential [C055]. The knife work is confined to the 14 comparison pages [C008]. They punch in SEO, not in public.

## 3. How they position their product

**Category frame.** Gusto self-defines in its homepage FAQ as "an all-in-one payroll and HR platform that helps businesses take care of their teams" [C002]. The About page anchors on meaning — "We're making work meaningful for everyone, everywhere" — calls the product a "small business platform," and discloses the 2012 ZenPayroll origin and "tens of billions of dollars of payroll" processed annually [C003]. Notably, this is a *narrowed* frame: the "People Platform" category Gusto invented at its September 2015 rebrand still persists on third-party listings like Product Hunt, but is absent from the current homepage — a quiet de-emphasis of their own invented category (inference; archives were unreachable for a hard diff) [C019].

**Claimed onlyness: none exists.** There is no onlyness sentence on the homepage, the payroll page, or the vs pages. Uniqueness is carried entirely by review-site superlatives — the vs-Rippling page opens with "Gusto is rated #1 in payroll | G2 Fall 2025," "#1 Most Innovative Company in HR 2025," "#1 Highest Satisfaction Software, G2 Review 2026," and a Newsweek/Statista listing [C004]. This is diffuse positioning: badges are cyclical, contestable, and not a structural story. Their proof pattern doubles down on it with small-n self-surveys [C005] and a deliberately operationalized review flywheel, including dedicated indexed pages on their own domain amplifying G2 reviews [C059][C058].

**Narrative arc.** The story is payroll-to-platform by accretion: more than half of ARR now comes from products beyond payroll by their own statement [C066]; Guideline has been rebranded as "Gusto 401(k), the best retirement offering for small businesses" [C072]; and the Mosey acquisition anchors a forthcoming "Gusto Business Compliance" offering [C070]. The arc is additive — more products for small businesses — not structural. Nothing in their language claims a unifying architecture the way Rippling's employee graph does.

**Named alternatives.** They maintain 14 comparison pages: Rippling, Wave, Paylocity, ADP, Justworks, Paycom, TriNet, Paychex, Square Payroll, Patriot, QuickBooks Payroll, BambooHR, OnPay, Homebase — payroll incumbents and SMB tools only, with no enterprise HCM (no Workday, no UKG) in the roster [C008]. Rippling gets the most aggressive treatment: an active switch-away pitch [C009] and a defensive FAQ that explicitly fields Rippling-frame questions — "Is Rippling better for IT and device management?", "Rippling modular vs Gusto bundled?", "Which is preferred by startups?" [C013]. That FAQ is a tell: they are defending against the HR+IT platform narrative and the startup segment rather than ignoring them.

**Target segments.** The solutions-by-size hub offers New businesses, Small businesses, Growing businesses, One employee (Gusto Solo), Contractor businesses, Remote & Global, and Multiple businesses — there is no enterprise or mid-market page at all [C017]. Customer proof matches: every featured story lists 7 to 61 employees (Handlebar Cyclery 7, Sleep Reset 11, Spiked Spin 15, Kids Atelier 61), drawn from accounting firms, dental and health practices, law firms, breweries, and consumer goods — zero enterprise or tech-scale logos [C016]. Accountants are elevated to a top-level navigation item — Products, Business types, For accountants, Resources, Pricing — signaling the channel's primacy in distribution [C007].

## 5. Pricing & packaging as positioning

Pricing is fully public, flat, and self-serve — the packaging itself is a positioning statement aimed at micro-SMB buyers who want no sales conversation [C037]. The tiers, corroborated across three independently fetched July-2026-current teardowns and (for Simple) Gusto's own vs page [C036][C014]:

| Tier | Price | What it gates |
|---|---|---|
| Simple | $49/mo base + $6/employee/mo [C036] | Single-state payroll only — multi-state is excluded [C042]; Time & Attendance Plus costs extra [C045] |
| Plus | $80/mo base + $12/employee/mo [C036] | The multi-state gate; hiring in a second state forces this tier [C042] |
| Premium | $180/mo base + $22/employee/mo [C036] | Performance reviews, dedicated CSM, dedicated/phone support, compliance alerts, certified HR experts [C043] |
| Contractor-only | $35/mo base + $6/contractor in active months [C014] | Base fee waived for first six months as a limited-time acquisition promo [C038] |
| EOR (legacy) | $699/employee/mo — raised from $599 effective March 15, 2026 [C040] | Closed to new customers May 1, 2026; new global customers referred to partner Remote, with Remote as legal employer [C041] |

The value metric is per-employee-per-month on a flat base, with no demo gate on core payroll and no public tier above Premium [C037][C043]. The free motion is not a time-boxed trial: "Gusto's payroll software is completely free until you hit the 'Run Payroll' button" [C046], reinforced by 50%-off-six-months offers running directly in TV creative [C022].

**What the packaging says:** they want self-serve small businesses that convert without sales touch, and they have structurally ceded everything above that — consistent with the missing enterprise pages [C017] and the micro-SMB proof wall [C016].

**The seams, which are campaign material:**

- The Simple base price rose from $40 to $49 effective March 1, 2026 — a 23% increase [C039]. EOR rose $599 to $699 two weeks later [C040], and then the product was closed to new customers entirely [C041].
- The multi-state gate is punitive by design: one employee in a second state moves the base from $49 to $80 and doubles the per-employee rate from $6 to $12 across the *whole* headcount [C042].
- Worked math from the teardowns: 10 employees on Simple is $109/mo ($10.90 effective per employee); at 30 employees the tiers run $229/mo Simple ($2,748/yr), $440/mo Plus ($5,280/yr), $840/mo Premium ($10,080/yr) [C044].
- Hidden costs sit outside the sticker: international contractor payments at $5/payment to US banks and international wires at a $15 minimum plus 1%–1.5% plus FX margin, state tax registration priced by state, next-day/same-day/instant pay priced per payroll, Time & Attendance Plus as an add-on on Simple, priority support upgrades, health-benefits administration billed monthly per eligible employee, and workers' comp administration [C045].

They weaponize this pricing against Rippling anyway: the vs-page table shows Gusto "Starting at $49/mo. + $6/mo. per user" against Rippling "Starting at $8/mo. per user / No transparent monthly pricing" [C011], and the search-surfaced framing runs "Rippling's modular, quote-based pricing is more customizable, but that flexibility comes at the cost of upfront clarity" [C047]. Honest note: this attack lands because the opacity critique is substantially true of quote-based pricing. The counter is §7's Play 4 — publish the math — not denial.

## 7. Positioning gaps & opportunities for Rippling

### (a) Where their story is weakest against Rippling's

**They have no structural story — and they know it.** No onlyness sentence exists anywhere on their core pages; their uniqueness rests on cyclical G2 badge cycles [C004] and small-n self-surveys [C005]. Rippling's "one employee graph across HR, IT, and Finance" is a structural claim Gusto cannot copy at any badge cycle — and their own vs-page FAQ shows they are already on the defensive against exactly this frame, fielding "Is Rippling better for IT and device management?" and "Which is preferred by startups?" [C013].

**Global is now a forced concession, not a fight.** Since May 1, 2026, any prospect with international employee ambitions cannot be served first-party by Gusto — new EOR business is referred to Remote, which becomes the legal employer [C041]. They raised the EOR price to $699 before exiting [C040], and what remains first-party is contractor payments in 120+ countries [C015]. Meanwhile their comparison content still circulates a "103+ countries vs. Rippling's 53+" line that is stale against their own exit [C048].

**Their anti-Rippling content is stale and partly refutable.** The vs page discloses its competitive data was "collected as of July 2025" — more than a year old as of this brief [C011]. Its flat assertion that "Rippling doesn't currently offer next day or 2nd day payroll" is contradicted by Rippling's own published same-day payroll capability via RTP/same-day ACH [C012]. And "no surprise fees" [C011] sits uneasily against their own add-on sheet — international wire minimums plus percentage plus FX, per-payroll fast-pay fees, state registration fees, per-employee benefits admin [C045] — a 23% base hike [C039], and a multi-state gate that doubles the per-employee rate [C042].

**Their pricing punishes exactly the customers who grow.** The gate math [C042], the worked tier costs at 30 employees [C044], and the March 2026 hikes [C039][C040] together invert their transparency story the moment a customer succeeds.

### (b) Segments and channels they underserve

- **Anyone growing past ~50 employees.** No segment page exists above "Growing businesses" [C017], the customer proof wall stops at 61 employees [C016], and the sales motion shows only first hires into mid-market with no enterprise cluster [C076]. The graduating customer has no story to graduate into.
- **Global-intent buyers.** Structurally unservable first-party since May 2026 [C041]; contractors only [C015]. This includes buyers who are US-only today but expect to hire abroad.
- **IT, device, and security buyers.** No product story at all — only a defensive FAQ entry [C013]. There is nothing to defend when Rippling bids on gusto-brand + IT/device/security terms.
- **Bottom-funnel comparison SERPs and performance social.** Their SERP defense is badge-titles [C032][C033], their comparison data is a year stale [C011], their performance creative is thin minimal-copy UI demos [C028], and 76% of TV is community-brand spend [C021] within an under-$100M total budget [C030]. This is winnable ground, and the GTM leadership build-out [C057] plus IPO-adjacent posture [C067] argue the window closes as they professionalize.

### (c) Campaign-ready plays

*Standing gates for every play below: (i) all Gusto price figures here are proxy-grade triangulation [C036][C044][C045] — confirm each number against Gusto's live pricing page on the day any public artifact ships; (ii) every named-competitor claim goes through comparative-advertising legal review; (iii) each play ships with a measurement plan — suggested primary KPIs: Play 1 graduation-segment pipeline sourced from /vs/gusto, Play 2 organic wins on fact-check queries, Play 3 global-intent qualifier conversion, Play 4 calculator completions-to-quote rate, Play 5 startup-segment brand-term share.*

**Play 1 — "Outgrown Gusto": own the graduation moment.** Target the specific triggers — the first out-of-state hire, the 25th-to-50th employee, the first IT or security requirement — with math built from Gusto's own gates: one second-state hire forces base $49 → $80 and doubles PEPM $6 → $12 across the whole headcount [C042]; at 30 employees that is $440/mo on Plus, $5,280/yr, before add-ons [C044][C045]. Artifacts: a /vs/gusto page anchored on the gate table, paid search against "gusto multi-state payroll" and "gusto price increase" (the 23% hike is searchable pain [C039]), and a proof-gap kicker — their customer stories stop at 61 employees [C016]; Rippling shows what employee 62 onward looks like.

**Play 2 — The fact-check counter-page: "Their data: July 2025."** A calm, dated, sourced rebuttal page. Lead with the refutation of "Rippling doesn't currently offer next day or 2nd day payroll" against Rippling's live same-day rails [C012]. Quote back their own disclosure that the comparison data was "collected as of July 2025" [C011]. Set their circulating "103+ countries vs. 53+" line [C048] against their own May 1, 2026 handoff of global customers to Remote [C041]. Tone discipline matters: Gusto never punches publicly [C055], so a factual, dated correction contrasts their SEO-only aggression without surrendering Rippling's posture. Two gates before this page ships: (1) the 103-vs-53 line is snippet-grade evidence — verify it still renders on their live page before quoting it (see §8); (2) the next-day-payroll refutation [C012] currently rests on Rippling's own published capability — have product confirm the live same-day rails and route the page through comparative-advertising legal review, since it carries the same public-fact-check exposure.

**Play 3 — The global disqualifier.** A one-question qualifier aimed at any segment with international intent: "Will you ever hire an employee outside the US?" If yes, Gusto's answer since May 1, 2026 is a referral to Remote as the legal employer [C041], after raising the EOR price to $699 [C040]; what remains is contractor payments only [C015]. Rippling's native global employment on the same employee graph is the structural contrast. Aim it squarely at "Remote & Global" — a segment page Gusto still actively markets [C017] — and at the remote-teams identity keyword they buy ads against [C027].

**Play 4 — The "true cost of simple" calculator.** An interactive calculator: headcount, states, payday speed, international contractors in; Gusto effective cost out — using the confirmed tier math [C044], the multi-state and Premium gates [C042][C043], the add-on sheet [C045], and the March 2026 hikes [C039][C040] — next to a Rippling quote. This is also the honest answer to their strongest attack: quote-based pricing genuinely lacks upfront clarity, and they are exploiting that [C011][C047]. The counter is value-per-dollar math and transparency moves, not denial. Publishing the math converts Rippling's weakness into the vehicle for their exposure.

**Play 5 — Defend and expand the startup beachhead.** Their FAQ asking "Which is preferred by startups?" is disclosed anxiety [C013], and their new-business machine is genuinely strong — Cofounder as an AI on-ramp [C053], the formations-at-2x macro narrative from Reeves's circuit [C056], free-until-first-payroll onboarding [C046]. So contest the slice where Gusto's model breaks earliest: venture-backed and tech startups, which hit multi-state [C042], device/IT provisioning [C013], and global hiring [C041] in year one. Bid on their brand plus "startup," "IT," and "device management" terms — they have no IT story to defend and no enterprise graduation path to promise [C017][C076].

### (d) What NOT to attack

- **Micro-SMB price and simplicity at single-state.** At 10 employees, Simple is $109/mo [C044]; under roughly 25 single-state employees Gusto wins on sticker, self-serve motion, and free onboarding [C036][C014][C046]. Fighting below the graduation line is a losing frame — seed that segment for later graduation instead.
- **Product love.** Roughly 12.8K G2 reviews at 4.6 stars [C058], deliberately operationalized as a marketing asset [C059], with the badge wall to match [C004]. Disputing customers' lived experience of simplicity reads false and helps them.
- **The accountant channel.** Top-nav placement [C007], People Advisory Certification [C061], the Gusto Next conference stack [C060], and now AI agents doing partners' own business development [C074] — this is a moat with real switching costs. Do not try to out-shout it; if anything, build a parallel advisor motion rather than a frontal assault.
- **Brand warmth.** The Oakland Roots program is 76% of their TV presence [C021][C025] and they never name competitors in public [C055]. Attacking their likability backfires; every Rippling counter should be factual, dated, and calm.
- **Their momentum.** $1B revenue [C065], majority-non-payroll ARR [C066], and a claimed 50K net-new customers a quarter [C064] are real signals even where self-reported. Position against their *trajectory for growing companies*, never against their health — "great until you grow" is credible; "failing" is not.
